📅 Timeline

Election Timeline

A typical general election follows this structured timeline from announcement to government formation.

Phase 1 — Announcement

Election Commission Announcement

The Election Commission announces election dates, schedules, and the Model Code of Conduct comes into immediate effect. Political parties begin preparations.

Phase 2 — Nominations

Filing of Nominations

Candidates file nomination papers with required documents and security deposits. Scrutiny of nominations follows, with a window for withdrawal.

Phase 3 — Campaigning

Campaign & Outreach

Candidates campaign through rallies, door-to-door visits, and media. Campaigning must stop 48 hours before polling ("silence period").

Phase 4 — Polling

Voting Day

Voters cast their ballots at designated polling stations using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs). VVPAT allows voters to verify their choice.

Phase 5 — Counting

Vote Counting & Results

Votes are counted at designated centers with representatives from all parties present. Results are declared constituency by constituency.

Phase 6 — Formation

Government Formation

The party or coalition with majority is invited to form the government. The leader takes oath as the head of government, and the cabinet is sworn in.

Your Rights as a Voter

Every citizen in a democracy has fundamental rights related to the electoral process. Understanding these rights empowers you to participate fully.

  • Right to Vote: Every citizen aged 18+ has the right to vote regardless of gender, caste, religion, or economic status.
  • Right to Information: You can access candidate affidavits, criminal records, assets, and educational qualifications.
  • Right to Secret Ballot: Your vote is completely secret. No one can force you to reveal whom you voted for.
  • Right to Complain: You can file complaints about electoral malpractice with the Election Commission.
  • NOTA Option: You can choose "None of the Above" if no candidate meets your expectations.
  • Accessibility: Polling stations must be accessible to persons with disabilities, with special provisions for assisted voting.

Electronic Voting Machines & VVPAT

Modern elections use technology to ensure accuracy and efficiency while maintaining the integrity of the voting process.

  • EVM (Electronic Voting Machine): A portable, battery-operated device that records votes electronically with a simple button press.
  • VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail): Prints a paper slip showing your vote, visible for 7 seconds for verification.
  • Tamper-Proof Design: EVMs are standalone devices, not connected to any network. They use one-time programmable chips.
  • Randomization: EVMs are randomly assigned to polling stations through multiple rounds of randomization.
  • Testing: First Level Checking (FLC) and mock polls are conducted before actual elections to verify functionality.

Types of Elections

Democratic nations conduct various types of elections at different levels of governance.

  • General Elections: Held to elect members to the national parliament/legislature. Typically every 5 years.
  • State Elections: Conducted to elect representatives to state/provincial legislatures.
  • Local Body Elections: Municipal corporations, city councils, panchayats — governing your local area.
  • By-Elections: Held when a seat becomes vacant due to resignation, death, or disqualification of a member.
  • Presidential Elections: In some democracies, the head of state is directly elected by citizens.
  • Referendums: Direct vote by citizens on specific policy issues or constitutional amendments.

Model Code of Conduct

The Model Code of Conduct is a set of guidelines for political parties and candidates during elections to ensure free and fair elections.

  • When Active: Comes into effect from the date of election announcement until results are declared.
  • Government Restrictions: Ruling party cannot announce new policies, projects, or grants during this period.
  • Campaign Rules: No appeals to caste or religious sentiments. No use of government machinery for campaigning.
  • Spending Limits: Candidates must stay within prescribed expenditure limits and maintain accounts.
  • Silence Period: No campaigning allowed 48 hours before polling to give voters time for reflection.
  • Media Guidelines: Exit polls cannot be broadcast until all phases of voting are complete.
